Hurb · Product Design

Checkout
Redesign

Travel has the highest cart abandonment rate in e-commerce. This is how we redesigned the last step of the funnel.

Client Hurb
Role Product Designer
Scope Research · UX Design · Usability Testing
Context

The last step is where travelers leave

The travel segment has the highest rate of cart abandonment in e-commerce by sector. The 3 main factors are research, price comparison and references with other travelers.

The goal we traced was to optimize the experience on the checkout page for the acquisition of hotels and vacation packages — reducing the huge page abandonment and increasing the conversion on the final step of the purchase funnel.

How to measure success
Bounce rate Conversion rate Number of reports on customer service
Process

Five stages

To conduct this project we followed our traditional design process, shared in 5 stages.

01

Discover

Analytics & BI

We did a quantitative research analyzing Google Analytics and BI to understand the numbers of the purchase funnel.

Hotjar

We analyzed through Hotjar the heatmaps and the video recordings of the user sessions on the checkout page, and gathered some patterns of frequent behaviors.

Stakeholder interviews

Interviews with stakeholders — customer service, commercial and marketing — to collect the pain points of travelers and hoteliers on the checkout step.

  • Doesn't find the coupon field.
  • Doesn't find the credit field.
  • Doesn't complete the second card on a purchase with 2 cards.
  • Doesn't understand the number of packages field on the overview.
  • Hotels that don't work with discount complain about the open coupon field.

Interface analysis

Analysis of the current page experience, collecting possible frictions and problems.

Confusing login area

  • Login button looks like a form field.
  • Facebook and Login buttons don't seem to have distinct actions.

Excess of visual elements and distractions

  • Big picture causing distraction.
  • Total cost, coupon and credits don't appear on the first fold.
  • Repetitive texts and long fields.
  • Incentives out of the purchase context.
  • Overload of information close to the CTA.
  • Credit field active when logged in without credits.
  • Small fields to touch.
  • CTA is far from the overview and final price.
02

Ideate

Affinity mapping

Categorization, in a collaborative technique, of the problems collected on the Discover step.

Grouped into 6 categories
Purchase overview Login Discount coupon Credits Payment Incentives

How might we…

Generation of questions in reference to the problems experienced, and voting for the best solutions.

  • Reduce the picture and texts of the overview, leaving price and fees visible?
  • Display the credit as inactive when logged in without credits?
  • Show the number of packages field without confusing the traveler?
  • Highlight the payment options and installments?
  • Make the login experience less confusing and in the segment standard?
  • Solve the price field problem on a purchase with 2 cards?
  • Display the coupon without highlighting the field?
  • Use the incentives contextually to the moment of purchase?

Solutions

  • New layout and positioning of the overview card.
  • Display the number of guests instead of packages.
  • Guest Default Login pattern.
  • Represent the coupon area via link.
  • Display the credit field just for logged users who own it.
  • Switch tabs to buttons and display the payment installment next to the price.
  • Empty price fields so the user is free to fill them out.
  • Synchronize the incentives with the user actions on the page.
  • Step checkout — "One Thing Per Page".

Design

Wireframe

Setting a responsive low-fidelity wireframe.

User interface

Interface with new UI and interactions.

04

Test

Test plan

Usability test planning using the usability test plan dashboard.

Guerrilla testing

Format
  • 1 moderator and 1 observer.
  • Stakeholder tests.
  • User and screen recording through the Screencastify extension.
  • High-fidelity prototypes, desktop and mobile.
Data tabulation
  • Grouping of objectives.
  • Success, problems and failures.
  • Notes and quotes from debrief.

Remote usability testing

Remote usability tests were applied to a selected user base through Maze.io. The test consisted of 3 tasks related to the analyzed categories and a qualitative quiz. Afterwards we analyzed the quantitative metrics provided by the tool — direct success, indirect success and bounce — the heatmaps and the quiz answers.

Test analysis

A new stage of categorizing problems and generating solutions that solve the issues faced.

UI alterations

After small layout modifications a new test was done, to compare with the previous metrics and validate the solution for development.

  • Highlight the purchase overview on mobile, emphasizing the click action.
  • Hide the step numbers.
  • Bring login, coupon and credits links closer to the step titles.
  • Modify the label and shape of the credits link for better understanding.
05

Deliver

Documentation

  • Delivery of the screens to the development team through Zeplin.io with screen specification.
  • Document with utilization scenarios, form field behavior and micro-interactions of the new interface.
Conclusions

What the project taught us

The quantitative and qualitative research were essential to understand traveler and hotelier behavior, gather real occurrences on the current checkout page and analyze standards adopted by the segment players.

An important factor in the efficiency of this project was the presence of data analysis in the other stages, not only at the initial Discover stage.

The use of fast, high-fidelity prototyping tools for the tests made quick adjustments to the interface possible before even going to development, optimizing the team's efficiency.

Small adjustments to the interface — such as the change of label and positioning — already improved the page usability, which will affect the conversion on the new checkout.

Let's keep exploring.
